#### Understanding hybridization dynamics in bulbuls in the Indian Subcontinent
Hybridisation and introgression are a potent evolutionary force in animals. Introgression, that is, the incorporation of one species’ DNA into a related species’ genome due to hybridisation followed by repeated backcrossing, has been implicated in many speciation events. In spite of hybrid zones being widespread in birds, and ten percent of bird species being known to hybridise, studies are limited from India. The widely sympatric red vented bulbul and the Himalayan bulbul are known to hybridise in regions where one species is locally rare. This project therefore pioneers the study of introgression in the Indian Subcontinent by building upon this and studying introgressive dynamics in bulbuls.

#### Understanding bird community composition across Delhi-NCR 
Delhi-NCR—one of the world's largest urban agglomerations by population—houses several urban green spaces such as parks, along with semi-wilderness areas such as the Delhi ridge. Several of these regions have also been restored and developed as biodiversity parks. This work uses citizen science eBird data to analyse how the taxonomic and functional diversity of this region’s bird communities varies spatiotemporally across these sites, along with identifying the potential drivers of these trends. 

#### Understanding the responses of tropical island endemic birds to climate change
Tropical islands’ geologic pasts make them important natural systems to study species responses to changing climate at both ecological and evolutionary timescales. Tropical island endemics represent either refugial populations, or in-situ diversifications. Moreover, being confined to singular islands, these species are exceptionally vulnerable to ongoing climate change. Using birds as a model system, this work uses publicly available genomes to understand the past responses of tropical island endemics to changing climate and identify the genomic signatures of endangerment in them. This is a collaborative project with Dr Balaji Chattopadhyay and Ratnesh Karjee from Ashoka University.

#### Genomic signatures of endangerment in Phasinidae 
This project investigates the genomic signatures of endangerment in the Phasianidae family to understand how genetic factors contribute to extinction risk in birds. By integrating newly generated high-quality genomes, publicly available genomic data, and whole-genome sequencing of museum specimens, this project examines patterns of genetic diversity, inbreeding, demographic history, genetic load, and transposable element dynamics across species spanning the full IUCN threat spectrum. Using a combination of short- and long-read sequencing, de novo genome assembly, functional annotation, and population genomic analyses, this project aims to test whether genomic indicators of extinction risk established in mammals are also applicable to birds. A particular focus is placed on threatened and critically endangered species, to uncover hidden genomic vulnerabilities that may not be captured by ecological assessments alone. This work seeks to bridge the gap between conservation genomics and extinction risk assessment, providing a genomic framework to inform conservation planning in Phasianidae and birds more broadly.

#### Endogenous Retroviruses as Contributors to Genetic Load
This project examines whether endogenous retroviruses (ERVs) contribute to genetic damage in genomes. Using existing gene annotations, this project identifies genes that contain ERV insertions within their coding regions and classifies genes as ERV-positive or ERV-negative. For these genes, it compares coding sequences across species and looks for signs of gene disruption such as frameshifts and premature stop codons. We shall statistically compare ERV-positive and ERV-negative genes to test whether genes containing ERVs show higher levels of loss-of-function, which would suggest that ERVs contribute to increased genetic load. This is a collaborative project with Dr Balaji Chattopadhyay from Ashoka University.
