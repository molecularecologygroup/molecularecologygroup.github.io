Durbadal holds a Master’s degree in Life Sciences (Bioinformatics) and a Bachelor’s degree in Genetics (Honours). His research primarily focuses on conservation genomics, with a strong interest in understanding species endangerment through genomic and evolutionary perspectives. He is particularly interested in studying how climate change influences genetic diversity, population structure, and long-term species survival. His previous research experience includes investigating genotoxic effects in Drosophila and conducting computational studies related to autoimmune diseases and SARS-CoV-2. He is passionate about applying computational and genomic tools to address questions in ecology, evolution, and biodiversity conservation, as well as exploring the dynamics of viral evolution. Outside of research, Durbadal enjoys playing cricket, football, and badminton. He also has a keen interest in photography, art, and music.
