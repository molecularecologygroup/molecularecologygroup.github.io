Principal Investigator

Dr. Kritika Garg is an evolutionary biologist specializing in population genetics and behavioral ecology. She joined IISER Mohali as an Assistant Professor in 2025. Her lab uses an interdisciplinary approach combining fresh and historic samples to understand speciation dynamics. She obtained her Master’s degree in Genomics from the Madurai Kamaraj University, after which she pursued her PhD in Behavioral Ecology at the National Centre for Biological Sciences (NCBS) under the guidance of Dr. Uma Ramakrishnan. During her PhD, she combined long-term field-based behavioral data with population genetic data and rigorous statistical modelling to define promiscuity and mating systems of natural populations. She provided one of the rare and earliest evidences of promiscuity in harem-forming bats, while also revealing the importance of flexible mating strategies in wild populations. After this she moved to the National University of Singapore (NUS) and worked on speciation and biogeography of various avian lineages. Her post-doctoral research focused on the effects of Pleistocene glaciations in shaping the avifaunal diversity of Southeast Asia and Australasia using both fresh and historical museum samples. Prior to joining IISER Mohali, Dr. Garg was a Faculty Fellow at the Institute of Bioinformatics and Applied Biotechnology (2020 to 2021) and Ashoka University (2021 to 2025).

PhD Student

Durbadal holds a Master’s degree in Life Sciences (Bioinformatics) and a Bachelor’s degree in Genetics (Honours). His research primarily focuses on conservation genomics, with a strong interest in understanding species endangerment through genomic and evolutionary perspectives. He is particularly interested in studying how climate change influences genetic diversity, population structure, and long-term species survival. His previous research experience includes investigating genotoxic effects in Drosophila and conducting computational studies related to autoimmune diseases and SARS-CoV-2. He is passionate about applying computational and genomic tools to address questions in ecology, evolution, and biodiversity conservation, as well as exploring the dynamics of viral evolution. Outside of research, Durbadal enjoys playing cricket, football, and badminton. He also has a keen interest in photography, art, and music.

Junior Research Fellow

Atri Bhattacharya completed his B.Sc. in Zoology from Ramakrishna Mission Vivekananda Centenary College (affiliated to WBSU) and his M.Sc. in Biology from Ashoka University, specializing in Behavioral Neuroscience. During his academic training, he worked on multiple research projects across different biological systems, including studies on sleep and infection in Drosophila and decision-making in zebrafish. His master’s thesis examined the effects of acute and chronic stressors on behavior and metabolic physiology in zebrafish, involving behavioral assays, physiological measurements, and statistical data analysis. He has also worked as a research intern in Dr. Poonam Thakur’s laboratory, gaining exposure to molecular and cellular neuroscience techniques, and in Dr. Ajoy Mallick’s laboratory, where he worked on experimental physiology and acquired hands-on training in basic bioinformatics. He is broadly interested in evolutionary biology, genomics, and bioinformatics, and is currently working on epidemiological data analysis and comparative genomic studies of retrotransposon accumulation in birds. Outside of academica, Atri enjoys music, badminton, chess, and occasional reading.
